# Academic CVs and human-sounding writing: research notes (Oct 2026)

These notes were compiled with web research and fed into `skills/academic-cv`, `skills/human-voice`, `skills/cover-letter` and the phrase list in `src/lib/checks.ts`.

## Academic CV

- **Faculty CV order (US research-intensive):** Education, Appointments, Publications, Grants, Awards, Invited Talks, Conference Presentations, Teaching, Mentoring, Service. ([The Professor Is In](https://theprofessorisin.com/2016/08/19/dr-karens-rules-of-the-academic-cv/))
- **Reorder by post type:** teaching-focused posts move Teaching up; keep several versions. ([Penn Career Services](https://careerservices.upenn.edu/application-materials-for-the-faculty-job-search/cvs-for-faculty-job-applications/))
- **PhD admissions:** Research Experience is read first (question, methods, your role, outputs); about 2 pages. ([DiscoverPhDs](https://www.discoverphds.com/advice/applying/cv-for-phd-application))
- **UK posts:** match evidence to the person specification; use "indicators of esteem"; referees only if asked. ([jobs.ac.uk](https://career-advice.jobs.ac.uk/cv-and-cover-letter-advice/academic-cv-guidelines/), [Cambridge](https://www.careers.cam.ac.uk/academic-applications-and-interviews))
- **Publications:** one citation style, your own name in bold, and separate peer-reviewed, preprint, under-review and in-preparation work. Never blur these categories. ([CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/scientific-curriculum-vitae))
- **Talks and teaching:** keep invited and conference talks separate, and mark poster vs oral. List teaching by course title and role, with no duty descriptions. ([Nature Careers](https://blogs.nature.com/naturejobs/2011/09/27/38-tips-on-writing-an-academic-cv/))
- **Header:** include your ORCID iD as a full URL. ([WSU Libraries](https://libguides.libraries.wsu.edu/scholarlyprofile/CV))
- **Biosketches:** the NIH Common Form biosketch has used per-field character limits since January 2026. ([NIH](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-26-018.html))
- **Academic cover letters:** 1–2 pages for STEM, up to 2–3 for humanities. Cover research (past and future), teaching with an example, and fit. ([Berkeley](https://www.career.berkeley.edu/grad-students-postdocs/academic-job-search/the-cover-letter/), [UNC](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/academic-cover-letters/))

## Signs of AI-written text

- **Overused vocabulary:** delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, intricate, meticulous, underscore, landscape, vibrant, garner, bolster, align with, foster, showcase. ([Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing))
- **Structural tics:**
  - "not just X, but Y"
  - trailing "-ing" clauses
  - groups of three
  - summary sentences at the end of paragraphs
  - title-case headings
- **Em dashes:** humans use about 2 per 10,000 words; AI uses about 17. ([Pangram](https://www.pangram.com/signs-of-ai-writing))
- **Measured phrase overuse:** GPTZero lists phrases that AI uses 10–200x more often than people. ([GPTZero](https://gptzero.me/ai-vocabulary))
- **Low burstiness:** AI writes sentences of uniform length. ([GPTZero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPTZero))

## Writing like a person

- Use plain words. ([plainlanguage.gov](https://www.plainlanguage.gov/guidelines/words/use-simple-words-phrases/))
- Give specific evidence instead of adjectives, and vary sentence length.
- Use contractions in letters and messages, but not in the CV.

## Recruiter attitudes and detector limits

- **TopResume (600 hiring managers):** 19.6% would reject AI-generated materials, while 52% accept AI help. ([TopResume](https://topresume.com/career-advice/ai-in-hiring-survey))
- **Greenhouse:** 74% of US job seekers use AI. ([Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair))
- **Detectors are unreliable:**
  - OpenAI withdrew its classifier, which caught 26% of AI text and flagged 9% of human text. ([source](https://synthedia.substack.com/p/openai-shuts-down-ai-written-text))
  - Detectors flagged 61% of essays by non-native English writers as AI. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819))

So Tailor's goal is **honest, specific writing in the candidate's own voice**, not beating detectors.
