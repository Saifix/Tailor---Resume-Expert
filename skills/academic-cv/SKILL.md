# Academic CV

Use this instead of resume-tailor's layout rules when the job is academic. **All of resume-tailor's hard rules still apply** (never invent, keep the identity block, plain Markdown, fact-check pass), and you write the same files: `resume.md` (the CV), `match-report.md`, `result.json` with keywords, questions, suggestions and — if web access is on — research.

## Header

Name, then one contact line: title/affiliation · email · phone (optional) · ORCID as a full URL · Google Scholar / personal site. No "Curriculum Vitae" heading, no photo, no date of birth (US/UK).

## Section order — choose by job type

- **Research-intensive faculty / postdoc / research scientist:** Education (dissertation title + advisor) → Appointments → Publications → Grants & Fellowships → Awards → Invited Talks → Conference Presentations → Teaching → Mentoring & Supervision → Service → (References, US only).
- **Teaching-focused posts:** move Teaching, Mentoring and Service up right after Appointments.
- **PhD admissions:** Research Interests (2 lines) → Education → Research Experience (question, methods, your role, outputs) → Publications/Posters → Teaching → Skills → Awards → Referees. ~2 pages.
- **UK posts:** a short key-achievements summary is acceptable; map evidence to each point of the person specification; "Indicators of esteem" (awards, fellowships, examining, editorial roles) is a common heading. Referees only if asked.
- Follow the field's conventions in the base CV when they differ.

## Section rules

- **Reverse chronology inside every section.** Years at the end of the line, consistent everywhere.
- **Publications:** one citation style throughout; **bold the candidate's name**; exact author order and venues. Separate *Peer-reviewed articles*, *Books & chapters*, *Proceedings*, *Preprints*, *Under review / submitted*, *In preparation* — never blur these (mixing unreviewed work into peer-reviewed lists is the most common CV misrepresentation). Only list "in preparation" items that exist in the base CV.
- **Talks:** Invited talks separate from conference presentations; mark poster vs oral.
- **Teaching:** course title (not number), role (instructor of record / TA), institution, term(s). No duty descriptions.
- **Grants:** funder, amount, years, role (PI / co-I), including small travel grants.
- **No prose:** no profile paragraphs, hobbies or undergraduate extras. Narrative belongs in the cover letter or research statement.
- **Length:** no page limit unless the ad sets one (e.g. 2-page CV, NIH/NSF biosketch, Europass) — then follow it exactly by cutting content, not shrinking text. Strongest material on page 1.

## Tailoring to the ad

- Mirror the ad's research areas, methods and teaching needs in section ordering and in which items you foreground (e.g. move the most relevant publications or courses to the top of their subsection only if the field allows non-chronological "selected" lists — label them "Selected publications").
- Keyword *have/added/missing* analysis works the same way; ask questions about real gaps (teaching experience in a required area, a method, a grant) instead of inventing.
- Never inflate roles (TA → instructor), venues, citation counts or impact factors.

## Before you finish

Fact-check every publication, date and role against the base CV and facts. Tailor checks that sections are kept, the identity block is unchanged, no new numbers appear, and the formatting is plain.
