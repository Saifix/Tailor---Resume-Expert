# Cover letter

Write `<job folder>/cover-letter.md`. Most hiring managers say they read cover letters but many spend under 30 seconds — so the first two lines have to earn the rest. They matter most for career changes, borderline fits and larger companies.

Inputs: the job description, the tailored resume (or base resume), `resumes/facts.md`, and any research notes (company links) you're given. Follow **human-voice** too if asked.

## Industry letter (default)

- **250–400 words, 3–4 short paragraphs, one page.** Plain Markdown: no headings, no bullets unless the JD invites them.
- **Greeting:** a named person if the JD or research names the hiring manager; otherwise "Dear Hiring Manager," or "Dear [Team] team,". Never "To Whom It May Concern" or "Dear Sir/Madam".
- **Paragraph 1 — the hook.** Name the role and company in the first sentence, plus one *specific, true* reason or proof: a referral, a result that maps to their top need, or a real company detail (product, initiative, recent news). Never "I am writing to express my interest…", "I am excited/thrilled to apply".
- **Paragraphs 2–3 — proof.** One or two short stories mapped to the JD's top 2–3 requirements. Add context the resume can't (why it was hard, what you decided, what changed) — don't repeat bullets. Use the resume's numbers exactly; never invent.
- **Why them** — one or two sentences of genuine fit, grounded in something specific from the JD or research. No flattery, no "dream company".
- **Career changers:** say plainly what transfers and why you're moving.
- **Close:** restate the fit in one line, say you'd welcome a conversation, thank them. No salary unless asked. Include a job reference number if the JD gives one.
- **Sign-off:** "Best," / "Thanks," / "Kind regards," then the candidate's full name exactly as on the resume.

## Academic letter (when the job is academic)

- **1–2 pages for STEM, up to 2–3 for humanities** (≈ 500–1,100 words).
- Structure: intro naming the post and the 2–3 strongest qualifications → research (current project, findings, and future agenda — say "current project", not "dissertation") → teaching with one concrete example → fit with *this* department (named faculty, centres, programmes from the ad or research) → close.
- Expand on the CV rather than repeating it. Don't call yourself "the ideal candidate". Make sure no other institution's name is left in the letter.

## Never

- Invent experience, numbers, names or company facts. If a detail isn't in your inputs, leave it out.
- Use stock phrases: "passionate", "proven track record", "I am confident that my skills make me an ideal fit", "unwavering commitment", "leverage", "synergy", "thank you for your time and consideration".
- Use more than one em dash, "not only… but also", or a summary sentence at the end of every paragraph.

Tailor checks the letter automatically (word count, no new numbers, AI-sounding phrases, greeting and sign-off) and may send you fixes. When done, reply with one short line.
